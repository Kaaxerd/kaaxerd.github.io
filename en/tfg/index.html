Bachelor's Thesis

Analysis and research of Pokémon Scarlet and Violet

1

The problem

Pokémon Scarlet and Violet (Game Freak, 2022) were the first open-world game in the main Pokémon series, running on the limited hardware of the Nintendo Switch. The shift to this kind of game exposed performance problems documented since launch by both the specialist press and players.

  • Memory leak Gradual performance loss after long sessions, caused by poor pointer management on the heap.
  • Pop-in / draw distance Sudden appearance of models and textures, caused by an overly aggressive culling algorithm.
  • Frame pacing FPS drops and stuttering in high-load areas, caused by poor CPU-GPU synchronization.
  • Netcode Latency and connection errors, especially in Tera Raids, caused by an unoptimized peer-to-peer architecture.

2

Question and objectives

What are the causes behind the poor performance of Pokémon Scarlet and Violet?

What solutions can be proposed based on software engineering?

This question leads to five specific objectives:

  • Extract and decompile the game executable legally, using reverse engineering tools such as Ghidra.
  • Classify by subsystem the functions of the main module and identify those with the greatest impact.
  • Analyze in depth the critical subsystems to detect problematic architectural patterns.
  • Measure empirically performance through frame timing and pop-in detection.
  • Propose alternatives for viable optimization, grounded in industry best practices.

3

Methodology

The diagnosis combines three axes that are cross-checked against each other to validate every finding.

Static analysis of the decompiled code Applied in chapter 4

Empirical profiling of performance Measured in chapter 5

Triangulation with sources Cross-checked in chapter 5

Legal methodology: no protected code is modified or distributed. Only the game's external behavior is observed (Art. 100.3 LPI, Spanish Intellectual Property Act) and cross-checked against verified secondary sources.

4

Development: findings by subsystem

Classifying the binary file main by functional subsystem identified the three components with the greatest impact on the game's performance.

Memory management

Problem: variable-size dynamic allocations with no segregation by type and no structured reuse, with incomplete release paths that fragment the heap.

Proposal: replace the scheme with pool allocators segregated by resource type, with constant-time allocation and release.

Streaming, culling and LOD

Problem: entity visibility is decided by rigid distance thresholds, coupled to immediately destroying the asset when it leaves range, with no spatial hysteresis.

Proposal: a FIFO queue of streaming requests with a per-frame loading budget and hysteresis bands between visibility states.

Performance and concurrency

Problem: game loop in a lockstep configuration, with logic and rendering coupled and rigid synchronization between CPU and GPU.

Proposal: a fixed timestep with an accumulator that decouples the simulation from the effective framerate.

5

Empirical results

Official patches improved the average framerate documented by the specialist press, but did not solve the underlying problem:

To go beyond this public data, I measured the observable performance in my own capture using frame rate, frame time (FFprobe) and indirect indicators of memory saturation, across three areas with different technical profiles, each designed to force a different bottleneck:

Artazon

8.42 σ FPS

Semi-open area of dense vegetation: it never stabilizes over the whole clip, showing the greatest variability of the three areas due to continuous LOD recalculation and frustum culling.

Casseroya Lake

56.1 → 32.4 FPS

Aquatic environment with active wildlife: the highest average of the three areas hides short, intense drops — the 1% low falls to 32.4 FPS due to atomic contention when assets activate.

Mesagoza

34.3%

The area with the most simultaneous entities: that 34.3% of duplicated frames and the 1 FPS minimums come from thread contention and blocking asset loading.

6

Conclusions

The performance problems of Pokémon Scarlet and Violet are not a consequence of the console's hardware limitations, but of architectural decisions in the engine.

The combined methodology of reverse engineering, profiling and source triangulation is transferable to any video game developed on hardware with limited resources.
